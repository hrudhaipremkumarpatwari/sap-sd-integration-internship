# Weekly report template

Use this outline as a content guide for each Word report. Tailor sections to the task and keep the report readable. Put the week number, exact task title, author, submission date, and version on the title page.

1. **Executive summary** — purpose, approach, key recommendations, and limits of evidence.
2. **Objective and task alignment** — map the supplied task objective and expected deliverable to this report.
3. **Context and scope** — process boundary, in-scope/out-of-scope items, intended audience.
4. **Assumptions and constraints** — identify assumptions and their impact; label estimates and simulated material.
5. **Method and sources** — research or analysis approach; cite sources close to relevant claims.
6. **Main analysis / design / test content** — use the week-specific checklist.
7. **Risks, controls, and contingencies** — where relevant, owner, trigger, response, and residual exposure.
8. **Recommendations and next steps** — actionable, prioritized, and traceable.
9. **Conclusion** — concise findings and limitations.
10. **References** — complete citations, access dates for web sources, and source-to-claim clarity.
11. **Appendices** — supporting tables, detailed cases, assumptions register, or diagram legends.

## Presentation and evidence

- Use descriptive headings, page numbers, table/figure numbers, captions, and a contents page for longer reports.
- Label conceptual diagrams **Proposed / illustrative architecture** and synthetic records **Synthetic example data**.
- For estimated or simulated values, include units, period, formula/source, assumptions, and a visible status label.
- Use a consistent date format and consistent system, message, and field names throughout the project.
- Avoid screenshots or diagrams that expose credentials, personal data, or proprietary materials.
